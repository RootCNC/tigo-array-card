# A complete solar dashboard, forecast tracking and savings

Everything here is optional, built around the Tigo Array Card. The files are in [`examples/`](../examples).

## The four-tab dashboard

[`examples/solar-dashboard.yaml`](../examples/solar-dashboard.yaml) is a full dashboard for a FoxESS hybrid inverter, Tigo optimisers and a Solcast forecast:

| Tab | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Animated power flow (solar → battery → house → grid), today's power chart with the Solcast forecast overlaid and battery SoC on a second axis, battery gauge, today's totals, a forecast row (today, remaining, tomorrow, peak) and today-vs-forecast / tomorrow charts |
| **Inverter** | PV1–PV4 inputs, MPPT chart, inverter and grid state, battery health (SoH, cell voltage spread, temperatures), and controls: work mode, SoC limits, charge and discharge power, import/export limits |
| **Panels** | The Tigo Array Card, full screen (Panel view type) |
| **Diagnostics** | Panels with no data, performance ranking, temperatures, RSSI, readings counts, firmware versions |

### Install

1. In HACS (Dashboard type), download **tigo-array-card**, **apexcharts-card**, **power-flow-card-plus** and **auto-entities**. Then reload the browser.
2. **Settings → Dashboards → Add dashboard → New dashboard from scratch.** Name it, then open it.
3. Click the pencil (Edit), then **⋮ → Raw configuration editor**. Replace everything with the file's contents, and save.
4. On the **Panels** tab, replace the roof card's YAML with your own layout.

Entity ids are the integrations' defaults. If a card shows "Entity not available", list your real ids with [`examples/home-assistant/list-entities.jinja`](../examples/home-assistant/list-entities.jinja) (paste it into **Settings → Tools → Template**) and adjust.

If every graph shows *"Custom element doesn't exist: apexcharts-card"*, the HACS card isn't loaded. Check it's downloaded, that it appears under **Settings → Dashboards → ⋮ → Resources**, and press Ctrl+F5.

## Solcast forecast vs actual

The Solcast integration's *forecast today* changes during the day as it re-forecasts, so to measure accuracy you need to **freeze a copy at fixed times**. [`examples/home-assistant/solcast-forecast-tracking.yaml`](../examples/home-assistant/solcast-forecast-tracking.yaml) adds five sensors:

| Sensor | Captured | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Solcast forecast at dawn | 03:00 | Solcast's figure for today, before sunrise (all year round) |
| Solcast forecast night before | 23:15 | The figure for tomorrow, which a night-charging automation would act on |
| Solar actual daily | 23:10 | What you actually generated |
| Solcast accuracy vs dawn / vs night before | 23:10 | Actual as a % of each forecast |

These are trigger-based **template sensors** in `configuration.yaml`. They don't need helpers or automations, and the long-term statistics are kept indefinitely. After a few weeks you'll have a correction factor such as "in November I get about 70 % of what Solcast predicts". It's worth checking your Solcast site settings (capacity, tilt, azimuth) if the gap is consistently large. On the free plan you can only define a limited number of rooftop sites, so a three-face roof may need two faces combined.

**Tips:**
- Paste the block under your existing `template:` key. Never add a second `template:` line.
- Run **Settings → Tools → YAML → Check configuration** before restarting.
- They show *unknown* until each trigger time first passes. The accuracy sensors need one full day.
- Template sensors have no automation trace. To confirm one fired, look at its *Last updated* time in **Tools → States**.
- The free Solcast API allows 10 calls a day. *API used 10/10* in the evening is normal; it resets at midnight UTC.

## How much is the system saving?

[`examples/home-assistant/system-savings.yaml`](../examples/home-assistant/system-savings.yaml) prices energy at the **rate in force when it was used**:

- **Cost without system:** everything the house used × the live rate.
- **Grid import cost:** everything imported × the live rate.
- **System savings today:** the difference.

Why not simply multiply solar generated by the price? Because solar stored in the battery at midday saves you money at the *evening* rate, battery losses are real, and once the battery is also charged from the grid overnight, "solar savings" stops being a clean idea. Measuring what you'd have paid against what you did pay handles all three. Expect the daily figure to dip **below zero overnight** while the battery charges from the grid, then climb back through the day.

Notes:
- Replace `RATE_SENSOR` with your tariff's current-rate sensor. The Octopus Energy one contains your meter serial and MPAN, so keep it out of anything you share.
- The `utility_meter:` section **only loads on a full restart**, not on a YAML reload. Until it has counted once, its daily sensors read *unknown*.
- Export income isn't included. Track it with the Energy dashboard's export compensation.

## Alert when an optimiser stops reporting

[`examples/home-assistant/panel-offline-alert.yaml`](../examples/home-assistant/panel-offline-alert.yaml) is an automation that checks every 30 minutes while the sun is above 10°. It raises a Home Assistant notification listing any panel with no update for over an hour, and clears the notification when they all report again.

## Toward automated night charging

The pieces above are the groundwork for charging the battery overnight only as much as tomorrow needs:

1. Take *Solcast forecast night before* × your measured accuracy factor.
2. Subtract a typical day's consumption.
3. Charge the battery by the shortfall during the cheap-rate window, using foxess_modbus's work mode, force-charge power and Min SoC controls.

Collect a few weeks of forecast-vs-actual data first. The accuracy factor varies a lot through the winter.
